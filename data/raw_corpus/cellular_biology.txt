Cellular biology examines the structural, biochemical, and physiological properties of cells, which are the fundamental units of all biological organisms. Organisms are classified into prokaryotes, such as bacteria and archaea that lack a membrane-enclosed nucleus, and eukaryotes, including plants, animals, and fungi, which contain organized membrane-bound nuclei and specialized organelles. The cell membrane consists of a phospholipid bilayer with embedded transport proteins that selectively regulate the passage of molecules into and out of the cytoplasm. Mitochondria generate chemical energy in the form of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) through cellular respiration and oxidative phosphorylation, functioning as the power plants of eukaryotic cells. The endoplasmic reticulum is categorized into rough endoplasmic reticulum, which is studded with ribosomes for protein synthesis, and smooth endoplasmic reticulum, which synthesizes lipids and phospholipids. The cell nucleus contains deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), which encodes genetic instructions across four nitrogenous nucleotide bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G). Mitosis is the process of cell division that results in two genetically identical diploid daughter cells.
