The periodic table organizes all known chemical elements in order of increasing atomic number. Hydrogen is the lightest and most abundant chemical element in the universe, possessing an atomic number of 1, standard atomic weight of 1.008, and three natural isotopes: protium, deuterium, and radioactive tritium. Helium is the second element with atomic number 2, characterized as a colorless noble gas with the lowest boiling point of any element at -268.93 degrees Celsius. Carbon has an atomic number of 6 and forms diverse allotropes with distinct physical properties, including diamond, graphite, and graphene. Gold is a dense transition metal with the chemical symbol Au, derived from the Latin word aurum, having an atomic number of 79 and a melting point of 1064.18 degrees Celsius. Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev created the first widely recognized periodic table in 1869, arranging elements by atomic mass and accurately predicting the properties of undiscovered elements. The modern periodic table contains 118 confirmed chemical elements, with Oganesson (symbol Og, atomic number 118) being the heaviest synthetic element officially recognized by IUPAC.
