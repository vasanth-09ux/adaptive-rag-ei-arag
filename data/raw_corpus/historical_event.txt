Apollo 11 was the historic American spaceflight mission that first landed humans on the Moon. Launched by NASA on July 16, 1969, from Kennedy Space Center in Florida, the spacecraft was propelled into space by the three-stage Saturn V rocket. The mission crew consisted of Commander Neil Armstrong, Command Module Pilot Michael Collins, and Lunar Module Pilot Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin. On July 20, 1969, the Lunar Module Eagle separated from the Command Module Columbia and touched down on the lunar surface in the Sea of Tranquility (Mare Tranquillitatis). On July 21, 1969, Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon, famously declaring: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Buzz Aldrin joined him nineteen minutes later. The two astronauts spent a total of 21 hours and 36 minutes on the lunar surface, conducting extravehicular activity for 2 hours and 31 minutes and collecting 21.55 kilograms of lunar rocks and soil samples. The Command Module Columbia safely splashed down in the Pacific Ocean on July 24, 1969, approximately 812 nautical miles southwest of Hawaii, where the crew was recovered by the aircraft carrier USS Hornet.
